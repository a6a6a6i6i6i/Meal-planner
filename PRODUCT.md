# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Personal daily tool — one person planning their own week's meals and tracking macros. Context is habitual and low-stress: a few minutes on Sunday evening or Monday morning to slot in meals and confirm macros are on target. The primary task on any given screen is: see the whole week at a glance, fill meal slots, verify macro totals. Speed and clarity matter more than onboarding polish; this user knows the app well.

## Product Purpose

A weekly meal planner with per-day macro tracking (calories, protein, fat, carbs). Users maintain a personal meal library, assign meals to breakfast/lunch/dinner slots across a 7-day grid, and get instant feedback on how the week's nutrition stacks up against their daily goals. Swap suggestions surface meals that better fit macro targets. Ingredient-level gram overrides let power users fine-tune without creating new meals.

Success looks like: opening the app, slotting in the week in under 2 minutes, closing it with confidence that macros are on track.

## Brand Personality

Warm · Playful · Delightful

The interface should feel like a well-designed kitchen notebook — not a clinical fitness tracker or a data dashboard. It should spark a little joy each time it opens. Inviting, personal, crafted.

## Anti-references

- **MyFitnessPal / calorie tracker apps**: Clinical gym-app energy, heavy grids, neon greens, progress rings everywhere. This app is warm and personal, not achievement-board aggressive.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards**: Navy sidebar, white card grids, cookie-cutter metrics layouts. Nothing that looks like it could be repurposed as a project management tool.

## Design Principles

1. **Cream-first warmth** — The canvas is always warm, never sterile white or cool gray. Warmth is carried through surface color, not just accent.
2. **Brand colors carry meaning** — Each macro has a fixed brand color (ochre=calories, pink=protein, mint=fat, peach=carbs, lavender=swap). Consistent everywhere macros appear.
3. **Today is the hero** — The current day column is the visual anchor of the week view. It should be unmistakably distinct without being garish.
4. **Delight in the details** — Small intentional touches (dot-coded goal pills, dashed empty slots, circular date badges) make a personal tool feel crafted.
5. **Clarity over density** — Macro data is only useful if it reads at a glance. Typography and spacing must prioritize scanability over information completeness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast against its background; large text ≥ 3:1. Light mode only — the cream canvas is central to the brand; dark mode is not in scope. No specific reduced-motion or color-blindness requirements stated, but brand colors should not rely solely on hue to convey meaning (bars use brand colors AND labels).
